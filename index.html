03Research

SOC Goal Incongruency

Current PhD Research

My doctoral research investigates the strategic coherence between Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs) and Security Operations Center (SOC) analysts — the SOC Goal Incongruency framework. I design quantitative risk metrics that measure risk mitigated versus activity volume, applied to CISA LME deployment in resource-constrained SOC environments.

Key Research Areas

SOC Goal Incongruency

Investigating why strategic alignment breaks down between CISO objectives and analyst monitoring practice — and designing frameworks to quantify and fix it.

Quantitative Security Metrics

Designing risk metrics that measure risk mitigated versus activity volume — moving SOCs beyond alert counts toward outcome-based performance measurement.

Detection Engineering

Applying CISA LME, Cribl, Wazuh, and ELK in resource-constrained environments to demonstrate enterprise-grade detection capability without enterprise budgets.

Agency Theory in Cybersecurity

Applying agency theory to understand principal-agent dynamics between CISOs and SOC analysts, and how incentive misalignment degrades security posture at scale.
